Information architecture

Organize around the decision, not the database.

Sentry groups product areas by analyst intent: triage, investigate, decide, manage rules, inspect customers, review operations and audit outcomes. Evidence modules stay inside the active investigation so analysts do not lose context.

03.02

Investigation object model

The IA distinguishes what is being reviewed from the evidence used to review it and the final disposition that becomes an audit record.

Alert

Prioritized risk signal with score, reason and time sensitivity.

→
Evidence set

Transaction, device, network, customer, beneficiary and rule context.

→
Analyst decision

Block, allow, verify, escalate or false positive.

→
Case / audit record

Rationale, actor, time, rule context and resulting account state.

03.03

Cross-cutting layers

Some systems should not become separate “screens” during every decision; they supply context across multiple product areas.

RULES

Policy context

Triggered rules, conflicts and precedence appear inside Alert Detail while the full Rules Engine remains available for policy management.

IDENTITY

Customer context

Customer history is embedded in the investigation and also accessible as a reusable 360° entity record.

AUDIT

Decision trace

Every committed outcome writes an immutable record without forcing analysts to manually navigate away from the queue.

03.05

Why the queue is home

The highest-value task is time-sensitive review. The architecture therefore opens on prioritized work rather than a KPI dashboard.

Design decision: analytics remain one click away, but the default landing surface is actionable risk work. This reduces the distance between arrival and investigation while keeping metrics separate from the evidence needed for an individual decision.
